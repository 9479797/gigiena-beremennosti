# 2. CONTENT DEPTH STANDARD

Always distinguish:

- `MENTIONED` — the topic is only named.
- `PRESENT` — relevant information exists.
- `EXPLAINED` — the reader understands what it is and why it matters.
- `ACTIONABLE` — the reader knows what to do.
- `COMPLETE` — the reader also knows what to do if the first step fails and when professional help is needed.

Never treat `MENTIONED` or `PRESENT` as equivalent to `EXPLAINED`, `ACTIONABLE`, or `COMPLETE`.

For substantial medical material, use the completeness frame:

> **WHAT → WHY → RECOGNITION → ACTION → NEXT STEP → SAFETY**

A definition alone is not necessarily an explanation.
A list alone is not an explanation.
A number alone is not guidance.
A recommendation alone is not an action plan.

For important numbers:

> **NUMBER → WHAT IT MEANS → CONTEXT → WHAT ACTION IT CHANGES**

---

# 3. SOURCE / EVIDENCE DISTINCTION

> **Source text ≠ medical evidence.**

> **Medical coverage brief ≠ final medical truth.**

> **Master editorial skill ≠ source of medical facts.**

Clinical guidelines and verified evidence determine medical validity.

The editorial skill determines how validated content is structured, explained, and written.

For a real source chapter, preserve useful source detail and identify:
- what is present;
- what must be preserved;
- what is shallow;
- what is only listed;
- what is unclear;
- what is duplicated;
- what claims require verification;
- what reader questions remain unanswered;
- what requires medical expansion.

Do not silently delete disputed or unsupported medical claims.

Distinguish:

- `SOURCE GAP` — important material absent from the source.
- `MEDICAL GAP` — material needed for medical/practical completeness and requiring research.
- `OPTIONAL DETAIL` — potentially nonessential detail.

---

# 4. EVIDENCE HIERARCHY

For a Russian patient-facing pregnancy book, prioritize:

1. Current Russian clinical guidelines / official national recommendations.
2. WHO where applicable.
3. Current relevant international professional-society guidelines.
4. High-quality systematic reviews / meta-analyses.
5. High-quality primary studies where necessary.
6. Reputable evidence-based patient resources for explanation only.

International guidance must not be imported into the Russian context without checking applicability.

Suggested evidence statuses:

- VERIFIED
- QUALIFIED
- CORRECTED
- EXPANDED
- UNRESOLVED
- SOURCE NEEDED
